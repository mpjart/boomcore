## Chapter 1: What Is Boom?

Boom is a fast tabletop RPG. Its rules are simple enough to learn in one sitting and open enough to run any story.

Boom stands for **Beyond Our Own Maximum**. Exploding dice carry you past the top of the die, and heroes grow past their limits.

### An Open Framework

These guidelines are an open framework for building the characters and stories you imagine, and for telling those stories together as a group. That is why Boom has **no classes, subclasses, races, species, religions, or any other built-in model** that hands out or takes away options for how you play. A knight, a pirate, a hacker, or something no one has named yet: if your Core Master allows it, you can play it.

### Three Goals

- **Fast:** few dice and little math, so combat and checks keep moving.
- **Simple:** six stats, one stat point per level, and one rule for exploding dice.
- **Open:** formulas, not lists. Where the rules are silent, the Core Master decides.

### What You Need

- These guidelines
- A Core Master (CM) and one or more players
- The Boom Dice app
- Imagination (snacks are optional, but strongly recommended)

### Theater of the Mind

Boom is built for theater of the mind: the Core Master describes the scene and everyone pictures it. There are no squares or hexes. If your table wants a map, keep movement loose and sketch it like a chalkboard. The Boom Dice app has one: the Map in the Dice tab, with a full-screen mode for a tablet or TV in the middle of the table.

### The Boom Rule

When a die explodes, throw out its result and roll **two dice of the same size** in its place. If a new die explodes, replace it the same way. A chain of explosions is a **boom**.

| Die size | Explodes on |
| --- | --- |
| d2 to d6 | a 1 |
| d7 to d20 | the minimum or the maximum |
| d21 and larger | a flat 10% chance |

**Example:** you roll a d6 and get a 1. It explodes into 2d6, which land on 1 and 4. Keep the 4. The 1 explodes into 2d6 again, which land on 2 and 3. Your total is 4 + 2 + 3 = 9.

**Boom cap:** one roll never uses more than 100 dice. At 100 dice, nothing else explodes and any remaining 1s or maximums count as rolled.

The app shows every roll to the whole party, one die at a time, so when someone's dice go wild, cheer for them. Yes, even if it's the Core Master. Especially if it's the Core Master; they need the encouragement.

## Chapter 2: Your Character

Every hero starts at **level 1** as a commoner, with no stat points and no abilities. You still have **3 ability slots**, and the Core Master can allow up to 3 abilities before level 2, such as a racial power (see Who You Are). Otherwise, your choices begin when you level up.

**Hit points (HP) = 5 × (level + Constitution points).** A level 1 commoner has 5 HP. A level 3 hero with 2 Constitution has 25 HP.

### Who You Are

**Your background is your story, not your stats.** Race, species, homeland, faith, and calling are flavor (see An Open Framework in Chapter 1). None is better than another, and none comes with built-in abilities like dark sight, flight, or a swim speed. What sets your hero apart is the stats you raise and the abilities you learn (Chapter 4). When the core is empowered, anyone can become a hero, no matter where they came from.

**Core Master note:** if you want a race to have a special ability, like water breathing, flight, or mental powers, assign it to the player as a **T1 ability**. It fills an ability slot and trains up one tier at a time, the same as every other ability.

### The Six Stats

- **Constitution:** HP, resisting effects and physical conditions, and protection magic like **lifeforce** auras.
- **Strength:** physical damage, and lifting, pushing, and breaking.
- **Intellect:** magic damage, and knowing and figuring things out.
- **Speed:** movement, actions per turn, and how long things take or how much you get done.
- **Charm:** glamor magic, and causing or resisting social pressure.
- **Spirit/Luck:** healing magic, luck, and the supernatural. It is the catch-all stat for checks.

### Leveling Up

Each level after the first gives you **one stat point** to spend on any stat.

The Core Master picks one way to level for the whole table, before play starts (in the app, Leveling in the CM tab's Table rules card):

**Milestones (recommended).** The Core Master levels the whole party at story moments, like finishing a quest, beating a boss, or a big twist. Nothing to track, and everyone stays the same level. In the app, one tap levels the whole party.

**The XP bar.** Every hero has an XP bar, and **only soul cores fill it**. Coins never buy levels, no matter how many you have stuffed in your boots.

- **A core of your level** fills 1/10 of the bar. Ten of them make a level.
- **A core above your level** fills the rest of the bar, so you gain exactly one level. Any extra power fizzles; you can't skip levels.
- **A core below your level** is too small to move the bar. Spend it on training (Chapter 4), or sell it if the Core Master allows.
- **Quest rewards:** cores don't only come from monsters. The Core Master can hand them out for finishing a quest, too.

**Example:** Mira is level 4 with her bar at 6/10. She eats a level 4 core and goes to 7/10. Then she eats the level 9 boss's core: the bar fills, she reaches level 5, and her new bar starts empty.

| Reaching level | Cores needed | Ability slots |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 10 level-1 | 3 |
| 3 | 10 level-2 | 3 |
| 4 | 10 level-3 | 3 |
| 5 | 10 level-4 | 4 |
| 6 | 10 level-5 | 4 |
| 7 | 10 level-6 | 4 |
| 8 | 10 level-7 | 4 |
| 9 | 10 level-8 | 4 |
| 10 | 10 level-9 | 5 |
| 11 | 10 level-10 | 5 |

Or one core of any higher level. Ability slots for every level up to 100 are in the Index.

**Ability slots:** 3, plus 1 every 5 levels up to level 20, then plus 1 every 10 levels. Leveling never grants abilities; see Chapter 4.

### Falling in Battle

**Level 2 and up:** if you drop below 1 HP, you **go down a level** and come back at full HP for that level. Extra damage does not carry over. Your XP bar empties, so you start the lower level from the bottom. You also lose a stat point (an unspent one first, otherwise a random stat). If you now have more abilities than slots, you lose one at random. Each fall costs another level, all the way down to level 1. Being knocked out, or any other effect, is a condition the Core Master applies.

**Level 1:** a level 1 character, whether they started there or fell there, **dies** on dropping below 1 HP. We recommend the party gets **24 hours** to resurrect them, or the player makes a new level 1 character. The Core Master can use any window they like. Resurrection can be an ability (like Return Spark in Chapter 4), a trainer's service, a quest, or an item like a resurrection scroll.

### Eating, Resting, and Healing

**Rest does not heal.** Short or long, a rest recovers no HP. If you are hurt, you need to be healed: see a healer, drink a potion, or receive a healing ability.

**Healing and temp HP.** You can only be healed if you are missing at least 1 HP. When you are, the full heal applies, from a spell, a healer, or a potion, and anything over your max HP becomes **temp HP**. Temp HP resets at dawn.

**A day starts at dawn.** Anything that works once a day, or resets daily, renews at sunrise.

You can also gain temp HP with an ability before or during a fight. For example, a Constitution (lifeforce) ability can be designed to grant temp HP or a shield.

**Commoners (level 1)** need food and about **10 hours of rest** every day. A commoner who goes without may fall ill, become malnourished, or grow weak, as conditions the Core Master applies. A day's food costs one spirit coin (Chapter 6).

**Heroes (level 2 and up)** have bodies that use everything with no waste. Consume **one spirit coin** and you need no food or sleep that day, and not even a trip to the toilet.

**Food buffs.** Food never restores HP, but the Core Master may let special food or drink grant a buff. A dwarven ale might make your beard grow, or elven cookies might double your movement for 10 minutes.

**Exhaustion** is optional, for Core Masters who want it. It is all or nothing, with no levels: you are exhausted or you are not. An exhausted hero **moves at half speed and can't work, fight, or cast spells**. You can still eat, drink, and take potions, but you are too worn out to do anything useful. **One 10-hour rest** clears it.

## Chapter 3: Combat

Combat runs in rounds. Everyone takes a turn in initiative order, and when the last turn ends, a new round begins.

### Initiative

When combat starts, everyone rolls **d20 + Speed**. Turns go from highest to lowest. Ties are settled by conceding (see Ties in Chapter 5).

**Holding your turn:** you can wait and act later in the round, even last, but you must act that round. Next round you return to your original spot.

### Your Turn

**Movement:** move up to **5 + level + Speed steps**, before your actions, after them, or split between them. A step is about one large stride; the Core Master has the final say on how far that goes.

**Actions:** each of these takes one action:

- Using an ability
- Attacking with one hand or both
- Interacting with an object, like drinking a potion or flipping a switch

You get **1 action, plus 1 more for every 4 points of Speed**.

| Speed points | Actions per turn |
| --- | --- |
| 0-3 | 1 |
| 4-7 | 2 |
| 8-11 | 3 |
| 12-15 | 4 |
| 16-19 | 5 |
| 20-23 | 6 |

**It's all just actions.** Boom has no reactions, bonus actions, or surprise actions to keep track of. Anything you do is an action.

**Saving actions:** you can use all of your actions on your turn, or use some and save the rest for later in the round. Use a saved action when you need it: throw up a shield, heal an ally, or wait for the monster that's hiding to show its cowardly face so you can shoot it. **Saved actions must be used by the end of the round, or they are lost.**

**Core Master note:** if saving actions slows combat down more than you like, don't allow it.

### Attacks and Damage

The dice depend only on how many hands (or limbs) you use and whether the attack is touch or ranged. The same chart covers weapons, spells, and unarmed attacks.

| Hands | Touch (melee) | Ranged |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One hand | 1d6 | 1d4 |
| Two hands | 2d6 | 2d4 |

- **Two hands is still one attack** and one action. Stabbing with two daggers is 2d6; throwing both at once is 2d4. Two different weapons each roll their own dice, all at once: a +1 dagger (2d6) and a plain dagger (1d6) attack for 3d6.
- **Sword and shield:** the sword rolls 1d6 and the shield takes an armor slot.
- **Unarmed:** a kick or headbutt is flavor and counts as one hand.
- **Range:** no range penalty. The Core Master sets the maximum distance. Smaller dice explode more, so ranged attacks boom more often.
- **Ammo:** keep a stack of more than one of any ammo (arrows, bullets, sling stones, power cells) in your dimensional storage, and it never runs out: take one out, and the stack refills. Bows, guns, and every other ranged weapon load themselves straight from your storage. Don't ask us to explain it; it's magic. It keeps combat fast, and that's the whole point. The refills are yours alone: hand one to a friend, drop it, or try anything but firing it from your own weapon, and it vanishes.
- **Magic** needs a free hand and a spoken word, unless the ability says otherwise. A one-handed spell needs one free hand and a two-handed spell needs both, so with a sword and shield you can't cast. This applies to spell abilities too. A spell that does anything beyond damage is an ability (Chapter 4).
- **Plus weapons:** a +N weapon rolls N extra copies of its dice (see Chapter 7).

### One Roll, Many Targets

Boom rolls as few times as it can.

- **Multi-target abilities roll once.** A Fireball that hits three targets rolls 3d6 one time, and every target takes that total, minus its own armor.
- **Minions swarm.** Minions of the same kind act together as one **swarm**: one initiative, one turn, and one attack roll. Pool every living minion's attack dice and add the bonus once. Four goblin minions with 2d6 + 1 melee roll **8d6 + 1** at one hero, and that hero's armor comes off once. To hit more than one hero, the Core Master splits the dice before rolling. Each minion keeps its own HP, so the swarm's dice shrink as minions drop. Five goblins, one roll, zero patience. (The app builds the swarm for you: same-kind minions get one Swarm card with pooled rolls.)

### Bonus Damage and Armor

Every attack is **physical** or **magical**, and adds a bonus. This applies to every roll that uses Strength or Intellect, including abilities:

- **Physical bonus = Strength − your magical armor**
- **Magic bonus = Intellect − your physical armor**

That is the trade-off of armor. A warrior in plate struggles to cast a fireball, and a mage in a warded robe hits softly with a staff. The bonus can drop below zero.

**No penalty for the other four stats.** A roll that uses Constitution, Speed, Charm, or Spirit/Luck adds the full stat, whatever armor you wear. That keeps healing, charms, smites, and lifeforce working as intended.

**Taking damage:** the attacker rolls damage, and your **total armor** (physical + magical) is subtracted from it. **Example:** 10 damage against 4 physical and 4 magical armor deals 2. Damage never goes below 0.

### Conditions

A condition is any effect the Core Master allows. The Core Master decides which conditions exist in their game and can invent any they need.

**Conditions never roll.** Use **T**, the tier of whatever caused the condition, as a flat number: T damage, T rounds in combat, and T steps of movement. A T3 condition deals 3 damage, lasts 3 rounds, and costs 3 steps. Outside combat, the Core Master decides how long it lasts. If nothing gives a tier, the Core Master picks one.

**"Every roll" means every roll:** attacks, abilities, healing, checks, initiative, and loot. No roll's total ever goes below 0.

| Condition | What it does (for T rounds) |
| --- | --- |
| Bleeding | Take T damage at the start of your turn. Armor doesn't stop it. Any healing ends it. |
| Blessed | +T to every roll you make. |
| Burning | Take T damage at the start of your turn. Spend an action to put it out. |
| Exhausted | Half movement; can't work, fight, or cast. Can still eat, drink, and take potions. Lasts until a 10-hour rest. |
| Frightened | You can't move toward the source or make touch attacks against it. |
| Invisible | Enemies can't target you unless they guess your spot. Ends when you attack. |
| Poisoned | −T to every roll. Healing magic or an antidote ends it. |
| Prone | Standing up costs T steps. Touch attacks on you deal +T; ranged attacks on you deal −T. |
| Slowed | −T steps of movement. |
| Stunned | Lose your turns: no movement, no actions. |
| Unconscious | Can't move or act. Ends when you are healed or the fight ends. |

## Chapter 4: Abilities

An ability is a saved roll that takes one action. "Ability" covers spells, techniques, gadgets, and anything else your story needs.

### Tiers

Every ability has a tier, the same six tiers used for loot. The tier sets everything, so the Core Master only remembers one number.

| Tier | Dice | Targets | Rounds | Effects | Feels like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 Common | 1d6 | 1 | 1 | 1 | A minor trick |
| T2 Uncommon | 2d6 | 2 | 2 | 2 | A solid move |
| T3 Rare | 3d6 | 3 | 3 | 3 | A signature move |
| T4 Epic | 4d6 | 4 | 4 | 4 | A battle-turning power |
| T5 Legendary | 5d6 | 5 | 5 | 5 | A hero's legend |
| T6 Divine | 6d6 | 6 | 6 | 6 | A miracle |

Outside combat, the Core Master sets how long an ability lasts.

### Building an Ability

1. **Pick a tier.** It sets the dice, targets, rounds, and number of effects.
2. **Pick a stat.** The Core Master chooses which of the six stats adds its points to the roll. Strength loses your magical armor and Intellect loses your physical armor, like any attack (Chapter 3). The other four stats add in full.
3. **Pick the roll's job:** deal damage, restore HP, or grant temporary HP.
4. **Pick the effects,** one per effect the tier allows:
   - **Condition:** applies a condition for the duration (Chapter 3).
   - **Move:** pushes, pulls, lifts, or teleports a target.
   - **Boost or drain:** raises or lowers a stat for the duration.
   - **Utility:** something the Core Master allows, like unlocking a door or talking to animals (fair warning: most animals only want to talk about snacks).

### Sample Abilities

Each sample is shown at full power for its tier.

| Ability | Tier | Dice | Stat | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spark Dart | T1 | 1d6 | Intellect | One target takes damage and is Burning. |
| Adrenaline Shot | T1 | 1d6 healing | Constitution | Heals one ally and Blesses them. |
| Mending Touch | T2 | 2d6 healing | Spirit/Luck | Heals two allies and Blesses them. |
| Stun Grenade | T2 | 2d6 | Strength | Two targets take damage and are Stunned. |
| Fireball | T3 | 3d6 | Intellect | Three targets take damage and are Burning, Frightened, and Prone. |
| Lifeforce Aura | T4 | 4d6 temp HP | Constitution | Four allies gain temporary HP and are Blessed. |
| Return Spark | T5 | 5d6 healing | Spirit/Luck | Revives a fallen ally within the resurrection window, with HP equal to the roll. |
| Reality Break | T6 | 6d6 | Intellect | Six targets take damage. The Core Master chooses what else bends. |

### Learning Abilities

Leveling never grants abilities. They come only from **ability tokens** and **trainers**, so the Core Master controls how they enter the game.

- **Slots:** you can know as many abilities as you have ability slots (Chapter 2). The limit keeps combat fast; nobody wants to wait while you flip through a 30-page spellbook. To make room, unlearn an ability; it is gone for good.
- **Start at T1:** every ability is learned at T1 and trained up one tier at a time.
- **Tokens and cores:** ability tokens have no tier and stack. A token does one of three things: learns a **new ability at T1** (with a T1 soul core or better), trains an ability **up one tier** (with a soul core of the new tier or better: T2 needs a T2 core, T6 a T6 core), or **mimics** a monster's signature ability once (below). A core's tier is the tier of the loot box it came from, so a level 5 hero only reaches strong abilities by beating rare monsters or getting lucky upgrades. A core spent on training does not fill the XP bar, so every core is a choice between leveling and training.
- **Trainers** do the learning and training. A coin tip or good roleplay can raise your standing and unlock better abilities. The Core Master can place a trainer anywhere or build a quest to find one.
- **Mimic:** spend a token to use the signature ability of the monster it came from, once. The Core Master rolls it, and the token is gone.

## Chapter 5: Stat Checks

When the outcome of an action is uncertain, the Core Master sets a target number and calls for a **stat check**. Roll **one d6 per point** in the stat that fits, add them up, and meet or beat the target.

With 0 points you roll a single d6 that does not explode. With 1 or more points the dice explode as usual.

### What Each Stat Checks

- **Constitution:** enduring and resisting. *Holding your breath through smoke to reach a door; weeding the tomato patch all day in the sun.*
- **Strength:** lifting, pushing, breaking. *Knocking a door down; hauling a crate of tomatoes to market.*
- **Intellect:** knowing and figuring out. *How a lock works; spotting which tomato plant has blight.*
- **Speed:** how long it takes and how much you get done. *How fast you pick a lock; how many tomatoes you pick before the storm.*
- **Charm:** persuading and resisting pressure. *Talking a guard into opening a door; haggling over tomatoes.*
- **Spirit/Luck:** the supernatural, seen as higher powers, karma, or plain luck, good or bad. *Picking the one door that isn't trapped; finding the one perfect tomato.* **If no stat fits, roll Spirit/Luck.**

### Setting the Target

Find the roller's stat points. **Easy** succeeds about 4 times in 5, **Medium** about half the time, and **Hard** about 1 time in 5. With 0 points, use Easy 2, Medium 4, Hard 6.

| Stat points | Dice | Average | Easy | Medium | Hard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1d6 | 5 | 3 | 5 | 7 |
| 2 | 2d6 | 10 | 7 | 9 | 12 |
| 3 | 3d6 | 15 | 11 | 14 | 18 |
| 4 | 4d6 | 20 | 15 | 19 | 24 |
| 5 | 5d6 | 25 | 19 | 24 | 30 |
| 6 | 6d6 | 30 | 24 | 29 | 35 |
| 7 | 7d6 | 35 | 28 | 34 | 41 |
| 8 | 8d6 | 40 | 33 | 39 | 46 |
| 9 | 9d6 | 45 | 37 | 44 | 52 |
| 10 | 10d6 | 50 | 42 | 49 | 57 |

The Index has the full chart with minimum, most likely, and maximum rolls. You can also set any number you like.

### Keeping Checks Fast

- **Set the number before the roll.**
- **Roll only when failure is interesting.** If nothing is at stake, say yes. Nobody needs a Strength check to open an unlocked door.
- **Failure changes the story,** like costing time or raising an alarm. It never stops the game.
- **Group rolls:** when the party tries something together, one roll decides it. The group or the Core Master picks who rolls, or the Core Master simply decides the group passes or fails.
- **No rerolls, ever, no passive checks,** and **no helping** unless the Core Master gives someone an ability that helps.

### Ties

Boom never rerolls, not even for a tie. When two rolls tie, someone **concedes**.

- **Core Master vs. player:** the Core Master concedes and the **player wins**. That covers anything the Core Master rolls for, like a monster, an NPC, or a trap.
- **Player vs. player:** the first player to concede is **the bigger person**, and the other player wins the tie. The bigger person **doubles the total of their next roll** (not the roll that tied). Someone has to be the bigger person.

**Example:** Ana and Bo tie at 14 on initiative. Bo concedes, so Ana goes first. On Bo's turn his 2d6 attack lands a 7, and being the bigger person makes it 14.

## Chapter 6: Spirit Coins and Loot

Money in the main setting is **spirit coins**. In another story it can be gold, bank notes, or credits; only the name changes. The Core Master picks the name for the table (in the app, the Currency name setting in the CM tab).

### Earning Coins

- **Labor:** anyone can make coins with time. One hour of work makes one coin, and one coin feeds a person for a day.
- **Daily limit:** at most 10 hours (10 coins) a day, then 10 hours of rest before working again.
- **Example:** a peasant family of four works 8 hours between them and makes 8 coins: 4 for food, 2 for shelter, and 2 for savings and taxes.
- **Heroes:** once a day, a hero can roll their Spirit/Luck dice (the same dice as a Spirit/Luck check) and make that many coins, in coins of their level's tier, without working. A new hero rolls one non-exploding d6, so they still need jobs.
- **Tithes and taxes:** optional. If your story uses them, the Core Master sets the amount.

### Coin Tiers

To keep numbers small, coins come in six tiers named like loot. Each tier covers a band of levels, and **one coin is worth 100 of the tier below**. Wages and food are always Common coins.

| Tier | Levels | One coin is worth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Common | 1-10 | 1 spirit coin |
| Uncommon | 11-20 | 100 spirit coins |
| Rare | 21-40 | 10,000 spirit coins |
| Epic | 41-60 | 1 million spirit coins |
| Legendary | 61-80 | 100 million spirit coins |
| Divine | 81-100 | 10 billion spirit coins |

Trade up every 100 coins of a tier for 1 coin of the next. To pay with smaller coins, break a bigger one and keep the change. The app does both for you. If you ever have to say "a hundred million" out loud, you're doing it wrong: trade up.

### The Loot Box

After a fight, a dungeon room, or a quest, each hero gets a **loot box**. (The whole box fits on one page: the [Loot Box Quick Reference](https://mpjart.github.io/boomcore/loot-box.html).) The Core Master makes the box (in the app, from the CM tab), and the player opens it; the app does the rolling.

**Coins:** roll **(monster level + your level)d6**, at least 2d6 and at most **20d6**, in coins of the monster's tier.

**Box tier:** the monster's rarity sets the box, from Common (T1) to Divine (T6). The Core Master can upgrade any box. The box tier sets item quality (Chapter 7), the tier of any soul cores, and the coins reagents are worth. A lucky box is lucky. The coin roll always uses the monster's coin tier.

**The d100 pool:** start with 1d100. Dice never add; each die is its own pull. Roll the whole pool, then resolve every die at the **highest tier the box reached**.

| d100 roll | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Boom:** no loot; the die becomes 2d100 |
| 2-50, even | A **sellable reagent** worth the number rolled in coins of the box's tier |
| 3-49, odd | A **healing potion** of the box's tier, in place of the reagent |
| 51-70 | An **ability token**, which can also mimic this monster's signature ability once (Chapter 4) |
| 71-99 | An **item:** d4 for type (1 weapon, 2 armor, 3 jewelry, 4 gem); from T2 up, d6 for its stat (1 Con, 2 Str, 3 Int, 4 Spd, 5 Charm, 6 Spirit/Luck). Gems never get a stat |
| 100 | **Upgrade:** no loot; the box goes up one tier (max T6) and the die becomes 2d100 |

**Naming loot:** roll a d100 clever name and a d100 base item, such as Black Hawk Longbow (+2 Speed). Mix tables freely: the clever name can come from one theme and the base item from another (a Space name on a Horror item), so any combination works, such as Space, Street Grit, and Grit 18+, or Horror and Fantasy. Gems get a quality word for the box tier instead, such as Polished Opal. Every weapon does the same base damage for its hands and reach, so the base item only sets hands, touch or ranged, and physical or magical. Grit 18+ is a bonus set of crude, punny names for adult tables, made to pair with Street Grit, and it mixes in like any other table. Use it only when everyone at the table is in (see Safety Tools in Chapter 8). In the app the Core Master toggles which tables a loot box uses when making it; with none on, names come from Core/Fantasy. The full tables for Fantasy, Pirate, Steampunk, Horror, Street Grit, Grit 18+, and Space are on the [Loot Name Tables](https://mpjart.github.io/boomcore/loot-names.html) page, and the app rolls them for you.

**Any even roll from 2 to 98** also drops a **soul core** of the monster's level, at the box's final tier. The core's level sets how much of your XP bar it fills; its tier sets which abilities it can train and what it sells for. The pool stops growing at 100 dice.

**Example:** Bob opens a T1 box from a level 3 monster and rolls 1, so he rolls 2d100: 100 and 64. The 100 upgrades the box to T2 and becomes 2 more dice: 38 and 79. At T2, the 64 is a token plus a T2 core, the 38 is a reagent worth 38 Uncommon coins (the box's tier) plus a T2 core, and the 79 is an item with +1 to a random stat.

### Using Loot

- **Sell** (only while the market is open, below): reagents for their number. Everything else (items, gems, potions, and cores, if the Core Master allows selling them) sells for a d100 roll, which can boom, in coins of the box tier it dropped at. A lucky box pays off.
- **Healing potions:** drinking one is an action and heals **one d6 per tier** (a T3 potion heals 3d6), and the dice explode on a 1 as normal. The normal healing rules apply: you must be missing HP, and anything over your max becomes temp HP. Potions stack by tier.
- **Consume:** cores fill your XP bar (Chapter 2). A token plus a core of the right tier learns or trains an ability (Chapter 4).
- **Bosses, quests, and dungeons:** see Party Loot in Chapter 8.

### The Market

The market is where heroes turn loot into coins and coins into rope. The Core Master opens and closes it whenever the story allows, like a trip to town between adventures. In the app, the **Market** button in the CM tab opens and closes it, and the roll feed notes each time. While it's closed, nobody buys or sells, and a player who tries is told the market is closed. The Core Master can always sell for anyone. This keeps heroes from cashing in a pile of loot in the middle of a dungeon.

- **What coins buy:** food, healing potions, adventuring gear (rope, lanterns, winter clothes, airship fuel), plain weapons and armor, and anything else the Core Master allows. Shops sell at fixed prices in coins of each item's tier and never buy their own stock back. The full lists, shop by shop, are on the [Shop Tables](https://mpjart.github.io/boomcore/shop-tables.html) page, and the app's **Shop** button sells from them while the market is open.
- **Shop weapons and armor are plain.** A shop weapon has no plus and rolls the usual dice for its hands and reach (Chapter 3), so a sword and a lead pipe hit the same; the look is flavor. Shop armor is always worth 1, physical or magical. Anything stronger comes from loot.
- **Cores:** for sale only if the Core Master allows. We recommend keeping them rare: one or two on a dusty shelf, or sold at auction to the highest bidder, so nobody buys a stack of levels.
- **Magic items are never for sale.** Heroes hunt for them or earn them as quest rewards.
- **The price dial:** the Core Master sets one percent for the whole table, starting at 0%. **Plus** means everything costs more and sells for more (a boomtown, a war, a dragon sleeping on the trade road). **Minus** means everything costs less and sells for less (a glut, a festival, a merchant who owes the party a favor). Apply it after any sale roll and round down: a d100 sale of 60 at +20% pays 72.
- **Keep shopping short.** A trip to market shouldn't eat a 4-hour session, unless the players and the Core Master really want it to.
- **Carrying your stuff:** every hero has **dimensional storage**, like a bag of holding, a pocket dimension, or a cargo drone, whatever fits the story, with room for everything they own. The Core Master can limit it however they like, such as only what you could carry, or no live animals. Putting one dimensional storage inside another, or inside a hole in space, does absolutely nothing. No explosion, no rift, no apocalypse. We know. Very boring for a game called Boom. Tracking your items is still the player's job. Your storage also keeps your ranged weapons loaded (see Ammo in Chapter 3).
- **No crafting or businesses.** Boom isn't built for crafting, running a shop, or anything like it. Other systems out there will happily let you play World of Business-craft. We highly suggest leaving crafting and business to the NPCs, while the players go out and be heroes, villains, or something in between. Just not NPCs.
- **No haggling rules.** The dial is the haggling. If the party sweet-talks the merchant, the Core Master nudges the dial. If your table really wants to play Used Car Salesman Simulator, we won't stop you: agree on a price, delete the item, and punch in the coins yourself.

## Chapter 7: Gear

There is no master list of weapons or armor. When loot or a story gives you gear, **the Core Master decides what it is**, and its tier sets how strong it is.

### Slots

You have **2 weapon slots, 2 armor slots, and 2 magic item slots**. What an item *does* decides its slot, not what it looks like:

- Deals damage → weapon slot (even jewelry)
- Gives armor → armor slot (a shield, or a mage's ring of shielding)
- Anything else → magic item slot

**Damage** means an attack: the swing or shot of a weapon, or the use of an ability. A bonus number added to that roll, like +2 from Strength, is not damage.

While equipped, an item's +N adds to the stat it names. Unequip it and the bonus goes with it. If the right slots are full, unequip something first. No, you can't wear a third ring on your toe. (Well, you can, but it won't do anything.)

### Gear by Tier

| Tier | Bonus | Armor per piece | Example magic item |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 Common | none | 1 | Lantern ring: lights a room (always on) |
| T2 Uncommon | +1 | 2 | Cloak of shadows: Invisible 2 rounds, once per fight |
| T3 Rare | +2 | 3 | Necklace: heals 3d6 when you drop below 2 HP, once per day |
| T4 Epic | +3 | 4 | Hover board: double your movement (always on) |
| T5 Legendary | +4 | 5 | Ring: +5 Speed for 5 rounds, once per day |
| T6 Divine | +5 | 6 | Compass that points to what you most desire |

### Weapons

A weapon can be a sword, a gun, punching gloves, an exploding football, or a broken bottle. Its damage comes only from hands and range (Chapter 3). Mark each weapon **physical or magical**, **one or two hands**, and **touch or ranged**.

**Plus weapons:** a +N weapon rolls **N extra copies** of its dice and adds +N to the stat its loot roll gave it. A +1 one-handed gun rolls 2d4, a +2 rolls 3d4, and a +1 two-handed 2d4 weapon rolls 4d4.

### Armor

Each piece is one number, equal to its tier, marked **physical or magical**. Two T3 pieces give 6 total armor. Remember the cost: each type lowers the other kind of bonus damage (Chapter 3).

### Magic Items and Jewelry

A magic item does one thing that isn't damage or armor. It never attacks on its own, but it can raise a number you already use: +N to a stat, or +N to your physical or magic damage bonus. That bonus rides on your weapon or ability roll; the item itself deals no damage. Healing and shielding items roll tier d6. Choose how often it works: always on for small effects, once per fight, or once per day for big ones. Use the table above as a guide.

### Gems

A gem is treasure to sell (see Using Loot in Chapter 6), with no stat bonus at any tier. The Core Master can also let a gem power an item or ability once.

## Chapter 8: The Core Master's Toolkit

Boom is a framework, not a rulebook. **Speed and fun are the goal of this system.** If a rule slows your table or doesn't fit your story, change it, drop it, or write your own.

### Safety Tools

Boom is about explosive fun, and it's only fun if everyone at the table is having it. Use these at every table, and always with Horror or Grit 18+.

- **Talk first.** Before the first game, agree on the tone (silly, gritty, or scary) and what the table is up for.
- **Lines and veils.** A **line** never shows up in the game. A **veil** can happen, but off screen: the scene fades out and the story moves on. Anyone can add either at any time, with no reason needed. Keep the list where the Core Master can see it.
- **The X-card.** Put a card with an X on it in the middle of the table (online, type X in chat). Anyone can tap it to skip or change what's happening, with no explanation and no questions. The Core Master edits the scene and play keeps going. It isn't a debate; it's a fast-forward button.
- **Check in.** After a heavy scene, a quick "everyone good?" goes a long way.
- **Grit 18+** is for adult tables where everyone has said yes. It's meant to be crude and punny, never cruel.

### Running the Game with the App

The Boom Dice app rolls and explodes the dice, does the math, and shows everyone's rolls. Only the Core Master sees the CM tab and the editing controls.

- **Table:** the Core Master starts a table and shares its 6-letter code; players join with the code. Everyone sees every roll live, revealed one die at a time so a boom chain plays out. **Play solo** runs a private table on one device.
- **Dice:** quick rolls, initiative, your color, the shared roll feed, and the Map (a chalkboard the Core Master controls, with a **Full screen** button and big text for a tablet or TV on the table). Tap the Boom logo to open these guidelines.
- **Party:** every character sheet. Collapsed sheets show the six stats at a glance. The Core Master edits everything; a player can equip, sell, and train on their own hero.
- **Loot:** loot boxes waiting to be opened, the party stash, and the **Shop** while the market is open. A hero's box is opened by its player; a group box has a timer and everyone grabs it.
- **CM:** the table theme (the look), the currency name, the **Table rules** (Milestones or the XP bar, the price dial, and whether cores sell), the Core Master screen, the Dawn and Market buttons, the loot box maker (with toggles for which loot name tables to mix), **One-shots**, handouts, save and load, and the monster generator, with swarm cards for minions.

**A round at the table:** the Core Master rolls initiative for everyone and the app sorts the order. On their turn, a player taps an attack or ability on their sheet. The Core Master applies the damage and taps Next.

### One-Shots

A one-shot kit is a ready-to-run adventure. Pick it under **One-shots** in the CM tab, choose 3 or 4 heroes, and every fight and loot box in it is one tap away: **Add fight** drops its monsters into the Monsters list with the adventure's stats, and **Make box** sends its loot boxes to the Loot tab. The first kit is **The Hollow Crown**, a three-session starter for levels 1 to 3 that teaches the whole game ([read it here](https://mpjart.github.io/boomcore/the-hollow-crown.pdf)). More are on the way.

### Party Loot

Normally each hero opens their own box. For **bosses** and **finishing a quest or dungeon**, roll one **party loot** box instead: **every hero gets a full copy** of everything in it, nothing split. Use the party's average level as the hero level in the coin roll.

### The Party Stash

The stash is shared treasure. The Core Master can add any gear, tokens, cores, or coins, or drop a loot box straight into it. Players trade by putting things in and taking things out; there is no direct giving. The Core Master can lock the stash at any time, such as mid-fight.

### Rewards and Pacing

With the XP bar, about half of all boxes hold a core, so a level takes roughly **20 boxes**. At four boxes a session, that's about five sessions per level.

- **Faster:** give a box per dungeon room or objective, use tougher monsters (one core above a hero's level is a full level), hand out cores as quest rewards, use party loot, or switch to milestones.
- **Slower:** give fewer boxes and let milestones carry the story.

**Lucky heroes:** a lucky box is meant to be lucky. If a hero gets too strong for the story, let a villain knock them down a few levels.

## Chapter 9: Encounters and Monsters

Fair doesn't mean easy. Fair means the players can see the risk and make a real choice. These numbers come from about 200,000 [simulated fights](https://mpjart.github.io/boomcore/encounter-sims.html) with parties of 3 to 5, so treat them as a starting point.

### Building Monsters

A monster is a simpler hero. The app's CM tab builds one from a name, level, type, and rarity.

| Type | Stat points | HP | Armor | Abilities | Threat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minion | (level − 1) ÷ 2 | half of 5 × (level + Con) | none | usually none | 1 |
| Standard | level − 1 | 5 × (level + Con) | level ÷ 5 | 1 | 2 |
| Boss | 2 × (level − 1) + 3 | triple 5 × (level + Con) | level ÷ 3 | 3 | 4 |

Round everything down, except a minion's HP, which rounds up.

- **Stats:** deal points one at a time with a d6: 1 Con, 2 Str, 3 Int, 4 Spd, 5 Charm, 6 Spirit/Luck.
- **Attacks:** melee 2d6 + Strength and ranged 2d4 + Intellect. At **level 1**, monsters attack with one hand, like the commoners they're fighting: 1d6 melee and 1d4 ranged. This is by design, to keep monsters quick to build. Flavor them to fit: a wolf bites with its melee attack and howls with its ranged one. Armor is one total, with no physical or magical split. The Core Master can always build outside this template.
- **Abilities:** built as in Chapter 4, at the monster's rarity tier, but **never above its level ÷ 3, rounded up**: T1 at levels 1 to 3, T2 at 4 to 6, T3 at 7 to 9, and so on. A level 4 Epic boss still uses T2 abilities. **A monster uses each ability once per fight**, then goes back to attacking. The first is its **signature ability**, which a token from it can mimic once.
- **Initiative and actions:** the same as a hero. Minions of the same kind swarm: one initiative and one pooled attack roll (Chapter 3).
- **Rarity** (Common to Divine) is a loose guide to toughness and sets its loot box tier. A boss is usually Epic.
- **Tier** is its coin tier, set by its level (Chapter 6). It sets the coins it drops. Its rarity, not its coin tier, sets the tier of its cores. Monsters carry no coins of their own; nobody wants to know where they'd keep them.

**Example, level 5 standard:** 4 points (1 Con, 2 Str, 1 Spd). HP 30, armor 1, melee 2d6 + 2, ranged 2d4, one ability.

**Example, level 5 boss:** 11 points (2 Con, 3 Str, 2 Int, 2 Spd, 1 Charm, 1 Spirit/Luck). HP 105, armor 1, melee 2d6 + 3, ranged 2d4 + 2, three abilities.

**Ready-made monsters:** the [Bestiary](https://mpjart.github.io/boomcore/bestiary.html) has over 130, built on this template and good at any level: crossover monsters that turn up in every theme (rats, angry bees, bandits, ghosts) and each theme's own, including every monster the app's New encounter button can make.

### Fair Encounters

Add up the monsters' threat points and compare with the party size.

| Difficulty | Threat per hero | Party of 4 | Example for 4 heroes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Easy | 1 | 4 | Two standard monsters |
| Fair | 1.5 | 6 | Three standard, or a boss and two minions |
| Hard | 2 | 8 | Four standard, or a boss and two standard |
| Deadly | 3 | 12 | A boss and four standard |

- Use monsters within a couple of levels of the party's average.
- **Low levels:** heroes have very little HP. At **level 1, quarter** the threat points (round to the nearest whole point, at least 1); at **levels 2 to 4, halve** them.
- **Small parties:** a party of 3 at levels 2 to 4 can't fit a boss into a Hard fight. Add a standard monster to keep it hard.
- **Armor:** it subtracts from every hit, so keep it at the levels above or fights drag.
- **Mid-fight:** if it goes badly, add reinforcements or lower a boss's HP. The app rolls the dice, so never fudge a roll.
- **Bosses** feel best with a couple of minions and a hazard in the room. A boss alone is just a very tall target.

**Need an encounter fast?** Roll a d100 on your theme's encounter table on the [Prep Tables](https://mpjart.github.io/boomcore/prep-tables.html) page. The higher the roll, the rarer the monster, so the roll also sets its loot box. The same page has rumors and plot hooks for every theme.

### Traps and Hazards

Build a hazard like an ability: its tier sets its dice, targets, rounds, and effects (Chapter 4). For example: T1 tripwire darts, T2 swinging blade, T3 collapsing floor (Prone), T4 poison gas (Poisoned), T5 lava vent (Burning), T6 a tear in reality (Stunned).

1. **Give a clue first.** Let the heroes notice something before it fires.
2. **Call for a check** to avoid it: Speed to dodge, Strength to push through, or Constitution to resist. Set the target with the table in Chapter 5.
3. **On a failure,** roll its dice once; every hero who failed takes that total, minus their own armor, and its effects. On a success, nothing happens.

## Chapter 10: Adapting Boom to Any Story

**Change the names, not the math.** Keep the Boom Rule, the damage chart, HP, the tiers, the level chart, and the loot box. That is what keeps the game fast in fantasy, space, pirates, horror, or a city street.

### Rename Anything

In the app, the table theme changes the look only (colors and fonts). The **currency name is the Core Master's call**: they set it once for the table (each theme's usual currency, gold, or any custom name), and everyone sees it, whatever look each player picks. **Soul cores are soul cores in every theme.** The other rows are optional names for the table to use out loud.

| Boom (Core) | Fantasy | Pirate | Steampunk | Horror | Street Grit | Space |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spirit coins | Spirit coins | Doubloons | Cogs | Grave coins | Bucks | Credits |
| Ability tokens | Ability tokens | Shanties | Blueprints | Rites | Favors | Data chips |
| Soul cores | Soul cores | Soul cores | Soul cores | Soul cores | Soul cores | Soul cores |
| Loot box | Chest | Sea chest | Steamer trunk | Coffer | Duffel bag | Crate |
| Constitution | Constitution | Sea legs | Fortitude | Grit | Toughness | Resilience |
| Strength | Strength | Brawn | Muscle | Strength | Strength | Might |
| Intellect | Intellect | Cunning | Ingenuity | Wits | Smarts | Tech |
| Speed | Speed | Swiftness | Dexterity | Agility | Agility | Reflex |
| Charm | Charm | Parley | Etiquette | Charisma | Swagger | Presence |
| Spirit/Luck | Spirit/Luck | Luck o' the sea | Aether | Fate | Luck | Fortune |

The Pirate theme covers ships, navies, and sea monsters: a weathered-deck look in the app, handouts drawn as grimy treasure maps (or pictures nailed to a black flag under a skull and crossbones), and its own [loot name tables](https://mpjart.github.io/boomcore/loot-names.html). Conditions might be Seasick, Overboard, or Keelhauled.

The Steampunk theme covers airships, clockwork, and gaslit cities: a dark leather and brass look, handouts typed onto leather in a riveted brass frame with gears, and its own loot name tables. Conditions might be Scalded, Overwound, or Short-Circuited.

Every theme has its own six shops on the [Shop Tables](https://mpjart.github.io/boomcore/shop-tables.html) page, from a bodega that somehow sells rope to a hangar that sells space stations (some assembly required). Every theme also has its own d100 rumors, encounters, and plot hooks on the [Prep Tables](https://mpjart.github.io/boomcore/prep-tables.html) page, from a sharksquid in the mill pond to a bodega cat that runs the neighborhood. The [Bestiary](https://mpjart.github.io/boomcore/bestiary.html) gives every theme its own monsters, plus crossover ones under local names: a bilge rat, a clockwork rat, and a vent rat are the same rat in a different hat.

### What Changes Easily

- **Abilities** can be spells, gadgets, implants, martial arts, or curses.
- **Magic** can swap the spoken word for a power source, or whatever fits the setting. The free-hand rule stays.
- **Weapons and armor** keep the same rules. A wrench is melee; armor might be plating and an energy field.
- **Conditions** fit the setting, like Overheated, Jammed, Spooked, or Seasick.
- **Monsters** can be aliens, gangs, ghosts, a rival crew, or a ship. Its guns are its attacks and its crew its abilities.
- **Leveling:** for a short game, level at milestones or stop at level 10 or 20.

### Tips

- Rename a few things first, not everything at once. By week two, nobody remembers that Constitution is now called Gumption.
- Keep the Core Master screen at the table as your cheat sheet.
- When the rules are silent, the Core Master decides and the story keeps moving.
